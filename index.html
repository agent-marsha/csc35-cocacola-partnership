The opening question

Coca-Cola has engineered the drink. The human at the machine is still an open question.

The next frontier is not the formula — it is the brain and body at the moment of choice: intent, attention and affect, served through machines that move in the real world.

Why now

Four frontiers, one shared problem: the human interface.

01 · Personalization

Drinks personalized by AI. The next step adapts to the person's state, not just their history.

02 · Connected dispensing

Freestyle machines keep getting smarter. The interface is still a screen we touch.

03 · Inclusive access

Hands-free control opens the machine to users who cannot touch it at all.

04 · Brand experience

Embodied hosts and measured engagement at live venues, instead of logo exposure.

Every one of these is a human-interface problem — and human interfaces are what we build.

Project A · Neuro-adaptive personalization

The responsive pour.

What we build

Dispensing that reads engagement and arousal from non-invasive EEG and physiological signals — never identity — and adapts the Freestyle moment in real time.

Why it matters to Coca-Cola

Personalization that responds to how a person actually feels, without covert sensing or stored profiles.

Project B · Accessible BCI control

The hands-free pour.

What we build

A brain- and body-controlled interface — attention, gaze and physiological signals — that lets a user select and pour without touching the machine, built on the low-cost sensing stack and universal-design method.

Why it matters to Coca-Cola

Serves motor-impaired and aging customers, sterile and sanitation-critical settings, and anyone whose hands are full — and puts Coca-Cola at the front of accessible beverage control.

Project C · Robot hosts at live venues

The living lab.

What we build

Embodied hosts — robotic and virtual — at sponsored venues, with physiological sensing to measure real engagement and test human-robot trust in hospitality.

Why it matters to Coca-Cola

Turns sponsorship from logo exposure into measured experience science that feeds back into products and venues.

Project D · Neurophysiological measurement

The experience signal.

A rigorous, ethics-governed instrument measuring attention and engagement using EEG and physiological signals — extending neuromarketing from the lab to the real point of choice, and giving Coca-Cola an honest, independent read on what resonates.

Project E · Low-code BCI & robot authoring

NeuroBlock Studio.

What we build

The block-based paradigm proven in NeuroBlock, rebuilt as an authoring toolkit so Coca-Cola's experiential and agency teams compose brain-driven and robot-driven experiences without a neuroscientist in the room.

Why it matters to Coca-Cola

Turns one-off activations into a reusable capability — faster ideation, lower cost, and a shared language between marketers and engineers.

Project F · Service & assistive robotics

The serving arm.

What we build

A BCI-driven robotic pour-and-hand-off system — assistive for users who cannot operate a dispenser, and a hospitality robot for high-traffic venues.

Why it matters to Coca-Cola

A credible entry into service robotics for beverage, with the human-factors evidence most robotics deployments skip.

The return

Peer-reviewed BCI and robotics results, co-owned under a negotiated agreement.

De-risked innovation

Small, evidence-backed pilots before any large commitment.

Credibility

Brain-interface and robotics claims backed by university research, with an ethics framework built in.

Talent

A pipeline to trained graduate researchers in BCI, HRI and physiological computing.

The model

How we would work together.

Funding

Deliverables

Quarterly findings, published papers, working BCI and robot prototypes, annual report to leadership.
